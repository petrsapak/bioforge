## Muscles don't move the creature (user: "ok now the movement is gone but the physics doesn't reflect that. If I move the muscles the organism stays in place")
- Measured, 10 game s, autonomous: a blob with a Pacemaker pulsing two back muscles moved 0.6 px; a tail with muscles on both sides 0.3 px; the Eel template 49 px; the Wiggler 2 px.
- Cause: water drag is linear, so a shape change that goes back and forth pushes the water as much one way as the other and nets nothing; small hex blobs also deform too little for the membrane's sideways drag to help.
- Tried and dropped: a drag on the membrane's stroke speed, measured against the body's own motion, as the square of the speed (0.02 to 0.15) and linear (10 to 80). The best gave the back-pulse blob 16 px in 10 s.
- Fix: Muscles kick. A contracting muscle pushes the creature toward its front from where it sits, `MUSCLE_KICK_FORCE` = 360 per unit of size lost per second (`physics.js`); relaxing pushes nothing. The cell type declares `kicks: true` (`data/cells.js`), and `actuators.js` records `contractionRate`.
- Result, 10 game s: back-pulse blob 216 px (about 22 px/s; two cilia do about 39 px/s), two-sided tail 65 px, Eel 63 px, Wiggler 130 px. One pulsed muscle on the right turns the creature left.
- Chosen without asking: the kick goes toward the creature's front, like cilia and siphons, rather than depending on where the muscle bends the body, so it is easy to predict.
- Golden fingerprints updated for the Wiggler, Eel and Crab (they have muscles).
- Tests: `tests/autonomy.js` checks that pulsed muscles on both sides kick the creature forward without turning (199 px in 5 game s), one on the right turns it left, and unsignalled muscles do nothing.
