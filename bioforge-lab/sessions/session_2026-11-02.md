## Sound effects came late (user: "It seems the SFX have a slight delay like 0,5 seconds after I click the sound appears")
- Not the files: every WAV in `audio/sfx/` starts within 3 ms (measured: first sample above -40 dB).
- Cause: each sound played through an `<audio>` element, rewound and started with `play()`, which goes through
  the browser's media pipeline. Web Audio, which plays a sound already decoded in memory, wasn't used because a
  page opened from disk can't `fetch` its own files.
- Fix: `audio/sfx/bank.js` holds the 18 WAVs that `data/sounds.js` names as base64 text (`SOUND_BANK`, 4.8 MB),
  loaded by `index.html` before `sound.js`. `sound.js` decodes them at start-up (`decodeSoundBank`) and plays
  them through Web Audio (`playBuffer`: a buffer source with the same pitch variation and a gain for the volume).
  The browser holds Web Audio paused until a press; `pointerdown` and `keydown` wake it before the click that
  follows, so the first button click is heard. Sounds while it is still paused are skipped, as before. The old
  `<audio>` path stays as the fallback (no Web Audio, or a file not decoded yet).
- `tests/soundbank.js` checks the bank matches the WAV files and that `index.html` loads it before `sound.js`;
  `node tests/soundbank.js --update` writes it again after a sound changes. `tests/load.js` skips scripts under
  `audio/`, so the other tests don't parse 4.8 MB each time they load the game.
- Checked in headless Edge: all 18 files decode, every effect plays through Web Audio, base latency 10 ms, no
  errors. The real delay can only be heard, so the user should try it.
- Chosen without asking: keeping the WAVs at their full quality (stereo, 44.1 kHz) in the bank; the music stays an
  `<audio>` element, since a loop doesn't need to start on time.
- PLAN.md: the sound effects decision and the architecture (`audio/`, the tests list).

## A Muscle body that doesn't swim (user: "if I replace them with tissue, then the creature is stuck in place"; "In reality the wave created by the body of the creature would move water behind it and push it forward, so I would say we need to revisit our physics")
- The user's Creature 3 (Muscles in three segments, each on its own 1.4 Hz Pacemaker with Delays of 0.5, 0.8 and
  1.1 s) swam 21.5 px/s only thanks to its Cilia; with Tissue in their place, 4.6 px/s.
- Cause: both sides of each segment squeeze together, so the body pulses instead of bending. With the right pair
  of each segment half a beat late (sides taking turns), it swam 28.4 px/s at 1.4 Hz, faster than the Jet.
- Water models tried in a scratchpad copy (20 s in open water, px/s head first): today's water (face drag 3 plus
  a square term 0.3, edge drag 0.3); linear only; linear with face drag 12. The bending body flipped to swimming
  backward at 2.2 Hz in all three (−18.6, −7.2, −12.9), because Delays in seconds make the wave's direction depend
  on the rate. The squeezing body stayed weak in all (at most 7 px/s). No physics changed.
- Proposed instead (not decided): the game makes the wave (sides take turns by themselves, each row a fixed share
  of a beat later), and a visible wake. The user asked for the Reference window and the Light eel next.

## Everything vanished in a crowded Sandbox (user: "When I started adding enemies to the sandbox screen. At some point the fps start to drop (around 25 enemies) and then everything disappears. going back to lab and back works this is not how the game should behave")
- Cause: a creature spawned into another gets a hard collision kick. Above about 1000 px/s the skin's square drag
  (0.3 per (px/s)²), applied explicitly over a 1/160 s physics substep, overshoots: the cell comes out faster the
  other way, and grows each substep to 1e178 px/s and then NaN. A NaN cell's box overlaps everything, so its NaN
  forces reach every creature in the same step, and nothing can be drawn. In Node: 9 Starters, 13 Eels or 8 Crabs
  clicked in at random places blew up; 60 Jets didn't.
- Fix (`physics.js`, `applySkinDrag`): drag may at most stop a cell within the substep, never throw it back. Below
  the cap the maths is exactly as before, so the golden fingerprints are unchanged and swim speeds too.
- Measured after the fix: 35 Crabs, Eels or Starters stay finite. In headless Edge, 30 Crabs (1364 cells): 10.9 ms
  sim and 7.4 ms drawing per frame, 21 fps shown (the rest is headless software painting); 40 Jets: 60 fps, 1.9 ms
  sim. The fps drop with many big enemies is load, not the bug.
- New test `tests/crowd.js`: a Crab thrown at 3000 px/s slows to 58 px/s in 1 s, and 12 Crabs clicked in one after
  another keep every number finite. Both checks fail on the old physics.
